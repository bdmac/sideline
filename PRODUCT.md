# Sideline product truth

## Purpose

Sideline is a mobile-first, installable game-day clipboard for youth recreational soccer coaches. It reduces attention cost during a match: the coach should be able to understand who is playing, who is waiting, how long each player has participated, and what the next fair substitution should be with a glance and a few large taps.

## Users and setting

- A volunteer or recreational coach standing outdoors in bright light, often using one hand.
- Coaches see only their assigned squads; one coach may manage both U8 playing 5v5 and U12 playing 9v9.
- Connectivity is not assumed. There is no account, backend, or cross-device sync.

## Durable behavior

- A fresh device begins with a static local coach picker rather than credentials or remote authentication. The selected coach persists locally until **Change coaches** is used. Brian is assigned to Golden Dragons as head coach and Fireballers as assistant coach; Chris is Fireballers head coach; Scott is a Fireballers assistant coach; Lindsey is a Golden Dragons assistant coach.
- Team selection shows only the selected coach’s assigned teams and keeps team state explicitly separate. Coaches assigned to one team skip this screen and enter that team directly. A coach assigned to an active game also resumes it directly, even when assigned to multiple teams; multi-team selection appears only when no authorized game is active. Its helper copy mentions starting a game normally and switches to resuming only while a game is in progress.
- Changing coaches clears only the local coach persona and never clears an active game, roster, preference, or history. While a game is active, coach-picker rows without access to that team remain visible but cannot be activated and name the team preventing entry. A stale persisted coach who is not assigned to the active team cannot resume or overwrite it; the fallback names the active team explicitly.
- The coach picker provides a named **End game** recovery action for the single active match. It requires destructive confirmation, finalizes elapsed time through the normal end-game path, clears the persisted active game, and opens the game summary with **Return to coaches**.
- Confirming **End game** definitively finalizes and removes the persisted active game before showing its summary. Leaving or signing out from that summary must never expose the completed match as resumable.
- The install-to-home-screen action lives on coach selection so it remains available even when a single-team coach normally skips team selection.
- When a game is active, team selection shows its current clock state and live soccer match time in the resume strip rather than a stale snapshot.
- Team names, rosters, and game durations are fixed product data.
- Game setup is a three-step linear flow for attendance, formation (including U8 period format), and starters/bench. Navigation buttons name their destination, and step selections remain intact when moving backward. Attendance tiles pair each player’s name with a compact jersey-number label for faster visual confirmation. Attendance below the team’s side size changes the count to a danger state and explicitly states the current count, required count, and shortfall.
- Starter assignment uses the selected formation as a tactics board. Tapping a position opens the present-player picker; choosing an assigned player swaps positions, while choosing a bench player replaces the current starter. Manual player choices and the Starting bench list are alphabetical for quick scanning. Auto-fill preserves manually assigned starters and optimizes the remaining present players only for open positions. When enough players and at least two eligible goalkeepers are present, initial auto-fill keeps one successor goalkeeper rested if every eligible keeper would otherwise start. It is disabled when the board is already full.
- Formations include an explicit goalkeeper plus the correct number of field positions. Every position has a compact code for spatial surfaces, a medium scan label, and a full descriptive name.
- The game clock can pause and resume. Persisted timestamps allow safe recovery after refresh or relaunch.
- Returning to a running game after Sideline was hidden immediately materializes elapsed time, persists the recovered clock, restores any added-time state, and surfaces an overdue substitution reminder without waiting for the next ordinary timer tick.
- Player field and bench time accrue only while the game clock runs.
- An opt-in, device-persisted **Demo mode** preference adds a fast-forward icon to the expanded live-game header. It opens a positive-minute offset dialog that first materializes any real elapsed time, uses the same field/bench accounting for the added interval, pauses automatically after the jump, and never changes periods automatically.
- Fair substitution suggestions prioritize players with the least total playing time coming in and players with the most playing time going out, so late arrivals are not penalized for having a short current bench stint. After at least one confirmed substitution, automatic suggestions project current Playing and Sitting intervals to the next scheduled rotation window while leaving real totals untouched; planning immediately after sending players in therefore models when the queued plan is expected to be used instead of treating every new entrant as permanently fresh. When several outgoing candidates are reasonably close in playing time, the planner spreads changes across defensive, midfield, and attacking lines and avoids replacing an entire line at once when possible. A substantial playing-time gap still takes precedence over line continuity.
- During live play, a reminder becomes due when too much scheduled game time passes without a completed substitution: 12.5% for U8 (5 minutes in a 40-minute game) and 25% for U12 (15 minutes in a 60-minute game). Sending players in for an OUT/IN substitution resets the hidden game-time tracker; an automatic bench replacement for a departing player also resets it because the on-field personnel changed. Merely readying a plan does not reset the timer. A ready plan replaces the no-plan reminder banner, but its countdown and due notification remain active. Pauses do not advance the timer, and period-break actions take precedence.
- When a running rotation timer crosses into due while Sideline is in the foreground and idle, the app opens the queued-plan review, or the suggested-plan editor when nothing is queued. This behavior has no separate setting and does not depend on sound being enabled. It never changes assignments, confirms substitutions, or pauses the clock. Open dialogs, menus, text entry, and pointer/keyboard interactions take precedence: a blocked or dismissed automatic opening is not retried during that rotation. Restoring an already-overdue game retains the reminder without a surprise dialog.
- Device-local game-day settings optionally keep the screen awake while an active game is visible and play one two-note chime per newly due rotation, with vibration where the browser supports it. Queued plans and open dialogs do not suppress that sound. Wake lock is a progressive enhancement: it releases when Sideline is hidden, reacquires on return, reports failure rather than pretending to be active, and remains disabled on unsupported browsers.
- Substitution reminders are team-level and do not require a goalkeeper change. Existing goalkeeper-reserve safeguards and coach overrides continue to control goalkeeper rotation without a second competing reminder timer.
- Goalkeeper-capable players use protected coverage rather than a permanently withheld bench reserve. At kickoff, Sideline keeps a rested successor when possible. Once the current keeper has completed part of the configured interval, a successor may be recommended into a declared outfield role when the remaining runway will not create an overlong uninterrupted turn. When keeper relief is due, a rested bench goalkeeper is preferred. If no eligible goalkeeper is on the bench, a sufficiently rested on-field goalkeeper can take over immediately through one atomic handoff: the current keeper goes OUT, the successor MOVES to GK, and one bench player comes IN at the successor's vacated outfield position. Coaches may override ordinary pairings, while the linked handoff remains atomic so field count, assignments, and bench membership cannot become invalid.
- Substitution planning begins with the goalkeeper-coverage maximum, then reduces the default at the first clear incoming-player fairness gap: at least two game-scaled bands of aggregate Played time, or two Sitting bands while aggregate Played remains comparable. The leading fairness cohort is always retained, a required goalkeeper change remains included, and a bench with no clear gap keeps the full recommendation. This prevents a large fixed count from forcing recently entered players straight back out while preserving full-line planning when the bench forms one comparable group. Changing the requested swap count re-optimizes untouched suggestions; once the coach has selected or readied specific pairs, count changes preserve those choices and add or trim only the surrounding suggestions.
- The multi-player planner uses a compact IN / OUT ledger. Player selectors stay concise while a separate status line shows the incoming player’s current bench stint and total playing time and the outgoing player’s current field stint and total playing time. IN choices rank least total playing time first, then longest current bench stint; OUT choices rank longest current field stint first, then greatest total playing time. Both use player name as the final tie-breaker. Players selected in another row remain visible but cannot be selected again in the same IN or OUT column, preventing duplicate plans before validation is needed. Every editable row keeps the affected position and substitution arrow between the columns plus an explicit remove control. Removing a row preserves every other selected pair, renumbers the plan, and lowers the requested swap count. The row itself is the complete planning summary; no duplicate confirmation ledger follows it.
- Planner player menus use one direct question rather than a title plus explanatory subtitle. The IN menu asks only **Who should go in?** because neither the current destination nor outgoing player affects the bench choice; both remain independently adjustable afterward. The OUT menu asks **Where should [incoming player] play?**, matching its position-selection purpose.
- Live substitution work is organized IN first, then destination, then OUT. The incoming player leads every planner and review row because the coach’s primary sideline task is getting the right player into the right position; the outgoing player remains explicit but supporting. Planner rows and the dedicated ready-review sheet use the medium scan label (for example, **Left Mid**) above the arrow, where the pitch’s spatial context is absent. The wider position lane deliberately takes space from the player selectors, whose names remain concise and directly editable.
- A confirmed substitution is atomic: no duplicate assignment, no player both on field and bench, and no accidental change in the valid field count.
- After a planned substitution or an automatic replacement for an on-field player taken out of the game, a substitution-ready sheet shows the numbered IN / OUT pairs and affected positions so the coach can organize players before play resumes.
- The latest confirmed substitution or out-of-game event can be undone.
- The game timeline is hidden until its first entry exists, then remains collapsed by default to protect live-screen space. Expanding it shows the affected player on out-of-game and return events and the OUT / IN players on substitutions. Every known event type has a stable scan icon: clock for period transitions, flag for the final whistle, reverse arrows for substitutions, four-way Move for position changes, soccer ball for goals, plus for returning players, and player-out for unavailable players. Structural quarter or half markers divide the event stream using soccer match timestamps: the first period starts at `0:00`, later-period markers begin at their nominal regulation boundary while pairing the newly started period with the prior period’s actual endpoint and added time, and the completed-game timeline marks the final whistle. These markers are not undoable game changes.
- Position changes do not count as substitutions. Tapping an occupied player magnet opens that player’s complete action sheet with the direction-aware **Plan out**, Change positions, and Take out of game actions. Bench-player planning uses the corresponding **Plan in** language. Choosing Change positions opens an editor fixed to that player with current-position context and large “Swap with” player/position choice rows matching the substitution pickers; the player cannot be changed inside the editor. Its confirmation remains disabled until a destination is selected, then reiterates the exact operation as **Swap with [player]** or **Move to [position]** before applying it. Dragging a magnet onto another position moves or swaps the players directly.
- Position changes from either interaction are recorded in the game timeline and can be undone as the latest event.
- The live game tracks a simple score. A coach records an own-team goal by choosing from the current outfield players (the active goalkeeper is omitted), or records an opponent goal without additional details. Goals carry the live timestamp, appear in the game timeline, participate in undo, persist with the active game, and produce the final score on the game summary.
- The live Goal action and Opponent scored action use Sideline's soccer-ball glyph rather than a generic add icon; the adjacent text distinguishes opening the scorer picker from recording an opponent goal. Individual scorer choices omit a leading icon so soccer balls within those rows exclusively communicate goals already scored.
- Each scorer choice pairs the jersey-numbered player name with their current position abbreviation in a compact neutral Label at the far edge. The Label prevents the code from reading as part of the name without increasing button height, while the accessible name includes the full position. Within this constrained grid, hat tricks compact to one hatted ball plus `×3`; roomier player markers retain all three hatted balls.
- Tapping the own-team score in either live header opens a compact scorer breakdown with one ball marker per recorded goal.
- During a game, the same ball markers directly follow scorer names across pitch magnets and roomy bench, out-of-game, scoring, position, and substitution rows. Dialog titles remain plain jersey-numbered identity and task labels so goal history never competes with navigation hierarchy. One or two goals use individual ball markers; an exact hat trick uses three hatted balls; higher totals collapse to one hatted ball plus the exact count. Native select options remain text-only where markers cannot render reliably.
- Match-level Start/Pause/Resume and End game controls live together in the expanded header and as adjacent icon controls in the collapsed header. Before the clock has first run, both header states use the plain-language **Start game** action; later period breaks retain specific actions such as **Start Q2** or **Start H2**. The mobile control dock contains exactly three equal actions ordered **Undo**, **Plan subs**, and **Goal**, keeping substitutions in the central thumb position. Position changes remain immediately accessible by tapping or dragging players on the pitch, so a separate Positions dock command is unnecessary.
- The primary live clock follows soccer’s continuous count-up convention across the whole match. Each later quarter or half begins at its nominal regulation boundary, so added time from an earlier period does not shift the displayed start of the next one; current-period added time still advances beyond that period’s nominal endpoint. A clock glyph sits beside the large value; the adjacent Start/Pause/Resume control communicates whether it is running, so the display does not repeat a textual clock-state badge. The period status separately shows time remaining in the current period and switches to an explicit `+m:ss added` state after that period’s regulation target. Actual accumulated active-play time remains authoritative for player totals, substitution fairness, chronological event ordering, and the final report.
- The expanded header names only the current period (`Quarter 2` or `Half 1`) without a redundant total-period denominator. On phones, it compacts into one sticky match-status row while scrolling, preserving the team, a concise `Q2` or `H1` marker, nominal soccer match time, score, and match controls. The ordinary period countdown is omitted from this constrained row; a secondary `+m:ss added` line appears only when the current period has exceeded regulation.
- Configured duration defines regulation time rather than a hard clock limit. At the end of a quarter or half, the clock, player field and bench totals, substitution cadence, goals, and timeline continue into explicitly labeled added time until the coach ends the period. The persistent period prompt absorbs substitution planning or ready-plan review so it does not compete with a second reminder banner. Ending an intermediate period materializes time and pauses the clock; the header and break banner then explicitly offer **Start** for the next quarter or half. Starting it establishes its regulation deadline from the actual restart, so added time shifts later boundaries without shortening later periods. At the final boundary, play continues in added time until the coach confirms End game. Refresh and relaunch preserve the active period, its actual start, and any overrun without truncating accumulated time. If an older recovery record lacks explicit period state, Sideline stays in the last acknowledged period rather than guessing from total elapsed time and accidentally skipping ahead.
- Taking any player out of the game requires confirmation. Confirming a bench player removes them from availability; confirming an on-field player then uses the fairest available bench replacement and shows the resulting change, or leaves the position explicitly open when no replacement exists.
- Players not marked as attending begin in the Out of game section rather than disappearing. A late arrival can be added to the game and joins the bench, or fills an open position automatically when the team is short-sided.
- During attendance, the coach can add named guest players borrowed for that match at any time, whether the team is short-sided or simply needs more substitutes. Adding a guest means they are present for this game; guest rows do not duplicate the permanent roster’s present/absent toggle, and removal is the single way to undo an addition before kickoff. The entry accepts an optional jersey number. Guests appear in a separate temporary section, participate in assignment, timing, substitutions, scoring, persistence, and the final report, but never enter the team’s permanent roster and disappear when the game is closed.
- If fewer than the regulation side size are available, assigning every available player enables an explicitly confirmed short-sided start rather than blocking the game.
- During a live game, the coach can add a game-only guest from the compact plus control beside the Bench count. The player joins the bench, their bench time begins at that moment, and the addition can be undone without counting as a substitution. During a short-sided game, tapping an open pitch position remains a shortcut that creates a guest directly in that position and starts their playing time immediately.
- The live Out of game list is absent when every player is available. Once populated, it is collapsed by default, describes its members as not currently available to play, shows its count in the summary row, and expands when a coach needs to add a player to the game.
- After 25% of regulation time, bench players below a 50% playing-time pace receive one compact warning. Bench rows prioritize identity and relevant timing exceptions. Players who have all sat since kickoff share one exact cohort timer above the list instead of repeating the game clock in every row; divergent sitting times return to the affected rows in `m:ss` below one minute and rounded values such as `4 min` afterward. Players with no accumulated field time read “Not played yet.” A single secondary-status line beneath the player’s history shows, in order of precedence, **Going in at [position] for [player]**, the below-pace warning, or distinct aggregate bench time phrased compactly as **22 min bench**. Planned on-field players read **Coming out for [player]**, and their aggregate field history uses the parallel **44 min played** wording. Both pending substitution statuses and planned-out pitch magnets use the recognizable reverse-arrows substitution icon; checkmarks remain reserved for selected or confirmed choices. Redundant duplicate bench totals remain hidden. Plan/edit and Take out of game remain direct actions.
- The live roster area has accessible Bench and On field tabs, defaults to Bench, and also supports horizontal swipe navigation. Bench rows sort by longest current sitting time first; On field rows sort by longest current playing time first. Equal times retain their prior bench or formation order for stability. On-field rows show position context and initially suppress **Playing** timing while every player’s current uninterrupted turn merely duplicates the main game clock. Once any current playing time diverges, every on-field row and pitch magnet gains comparable numeric timing in `m:ss` below one minute and rounded values such as `4 min` afterward. Pitch magnets deliberately show current **Playing** time rather than aggregate **Played** time because the field is the immediate rest-and-rotation surface; aggregate fairness remains available in both roster lists and player dialogs. Pitch magnets also add the substitution swap icon beside the position code when that player is ready to come out. Their **Plan out** action starts with the outgoing player and asks which bench player should enter. Tapping a pitch player opens a shared player-context panel followed by a compact three-action rail for Plan out, Positions, and Take out.
- When a player added to the game fills an open position, a player-ready sheet identifies the numbered player and their assigned position before play continues.
- Ending a game pauses and materializes the clock, scrolls the report to the top, then shows a per-player summary sorted with scorers first and remaining players by total playing time. It includes every position played, time at each position, total playing time, and total goals. The game timeline records the position occupied for each goal alongside its exact scoring time. The same confirmed-event timeline appears below the player summary in a collapsed disclosure.
- Substitution planning is a persistent two-phase workflow. Readying a plan does not change the lineup or timers; the live screen keeps the ready plan visible and editable until the coach sends every swap in atomically at the actual substitution time. Suggestions are projected when generated, then remain a stable coach-approved snapshot rather than silently re-optimizing while queued. A ready swap belongs to its OUT / IN player pairing: if the outgoing player changes position before the substitution, the plan follows that player to the new position without changing who goes out or in. Undoing the position change restores the prior planned position. The review screen can remove individual swaps without creating empty replacement rows: removing one reduces the intended plan size by one, and removing the final swap deletes the plan. Goal markers are deliberately omitted from this operational checklist so names, jersey numbers, direction, and position remain the only staging information. Plans made impossible by other lineup changes remain visible but cannot be sent in until repaired or cancelled.
- Player selectors inside the multi-substitution planner use the same row anatomy as focused Plan in and Plan out dialogs. IN choices show numbered identity beside explicit preferred roles with Bench / Played timing; they omit ordinal fit because **Who should go in?** is intentionally independent of the row’s changeable destination. OUT choices lead with position and preference rank, then show jersey-numbered identity with Playing / Total timing. Focused Plan in and planner OUT destination lists share one deterministic order: unassigned positions first; rest-due players whose current Playing time reaches the configured rotation interval; normal players; then fresh players below one completed game-scaled fairness band. Within the same rest zone, aggregate Played-time bands lead, with a one-band adjustment for outside-preference destinations, followed by current Playing-time bands, ordinal preference rank, exact timing, and formation order. The band is 20% of the game’s recommended rotation interval, rounded to a whole minute with a two-minute minimum—two minutes for default U8 games and three minutes for default U12 games. This prevents insignificant clock differences from reshuffling recommendations, protects someone who just entered, and promotes someone who has played continuously for too long without abandoning whole-game fairness. Every OUT position remains visible: positions already assigned to another row move after unplanned choices, show **Going out for [incoming player]**, and selecting one swaps the two rows’ OUT destinations so the plan remains complete and duplicate-free. Incoming players already used elsewhere remain visible with concise reverse-arrows **Going in for [player]** status, sort after unplanned choices, and can be safely transferred between swaps. Focused pickers retain pairing context for transferable choices in either direction.
- When planning a bench player IN, destination choices lead with the full position name and show the current player’s numbered identity as secondary context. The planner’s **Who’s coming OUT?** menu uses the same position-first hierarchy. Planning a single on-field player OUT keeps the incoming player’s name primary because that focused task begins with a known position and outgoing player.
- Each available bench player has a direct plan-in action. Its title explicitly identifies that the player is going in, and a responsive fact grid shows **Sitting now**, **Played**, preferred roles, and the next-rotation pairing when one already exists. Its outgoing-player choices show position, **Playing** time for the current uninterrupted turn, and total played so the coach can compare workload before selecting who comes off. On-field variants, including the player-actions, plan-out, and change-position dialogs, use the same grid with separate **Position**, **Played**, and **Playing now** facts rather than combining multiple times in one sentence. All player-specific time facts and choice-row metrics use `m:ss` only below one minute, then stable rounded values such as `9 min`, preventing live dialogs from visually changing every second. A zero lifetime playing total reads **Not played yet**; active playing and sitting timers retain `0:00` because they begin counting when play resumes. They also show the same conditional **Next rotation** pairing, so moving a player never hides relevant ready-plan context. Focused plan-in, plan-out, and change-position dialogs all use the same select-then-confirm interaction and two-line choice-row anatomy: identity opposite role fit or destination, then compact labeled timing beneath. Plan-out candidate rows use their lower-left space for a distinct **PREFERS** label and role value, explaining the ordinal or outside-preferences result while bench and played time remain aligned opposite. A single preferred role remains spelled out; multiple roles compact to **GK / DEF / MID / FWD** abbreviations while assistive text retains the full names. Preference matches retain explicit ordinal labels and add a restrained rank tint: green for first, gold for second, orange for third, rust for fourth, and red for **Outside preferences**. The words remain the primary signal, so color never carries the meaning alone. Selecting a destination in Change positions previews the Card value as the current full position, a swap arrow, and the destination abbreviation before confirmation. Its disabled footer instructs **Choose a position**, then becomes the specific **Swap with…** or **Move to…** action. Tapping a row previews the selection without changing persistent game state, the check-marked footer action commits it, and the header close control cancels. Open destination positions remain sparse and explicitly say **Unoccupied position**. Selected rows use a checkmark, turf surface, and structural inset in addition to `aria-pressed`. Confirming the focused substitution starts a ready plan or updates that player within the existing ready batch without rebuilding unrelated swaps, then returns directly to the live game rather than opening the full checklist. Review remains available explicitly or through the guarded automatic opening at the next rotation deadline.
- Individual player dialogs consistently lead with the jersey-numbered identity, followed by the focused task after a hyphen: **#10 Simon**, **#10 Simon - Plan out**, or **#10 Simon - Change position**. Their context Cards contain only game-state facts and never repeat the player name.
- Taking a ready bench player out of the game automatically removes that player’s swap while preserving every unaffected pair in the plan. Undoing the change restores the prior plan.
- Ending an active game requires confirmation. Its safe action is **Continue game**, and both that action and the standard close button dismiss the dialog without changing the game.
- The home screen offers installation whenever Sideline is running in a browser. Supported browsers open their native install prompt; iPhone and iPad users receive concise Add to Home Screen instructions.

Auto-fill maximizes these preferences deterministically while preserving a rested goalkeeper successor at kickoff when the roster permits it. Substitution planning wraps aggregate Played-time fairness in current-Playing guardrails. Players at or beyond the configured rotation interval are rest due and receive first priority; players below one completed fairness band are fresh and remain protected when enough normal or rest-due choices exist. Within the normal zone, aggregate Played-time bands are primary, with the existing one-band outside-preference adjustment preventing a marginal fairness difference from forcing a poor role; current Playing-time bands then settle comparable adjusted totals. Automatic goalkeeper changes occur only between players who declare goalkeeper as a preferred role; the general optimizer never assigns an ordinary incoming player to goal. Goalkeeper coverage may stage a future keeper in a declared outfield role once there is safe runway before the next keeper change. At the keeper interval, Sideline prefers a rested bench keeper; otherwise it can atomically move a sufficiently rested on-field keeper to GK while replacing the vacated outfield position and benching the current keeper. After rest zone and adjusted workload, the planner uses bounded line-continuity penalties to settle equally fair groups, then ordinal preferences and exact times as deterministic tie-breakers. The result protects recent entrants, distributes total minutes fairly during normal play, and forces rest when someone has played continuously for too long.

## Data and privacy

All data is stored in browser `localStorage`. Roster and game state use a versioned key; light/dark appearance and game-day device preferences use separate keys. Sideline does not transmit names or game data. Clearing browser site data removes the data and preferences.

## MVP boundaries

The MVP does not include authentication, a backend, cloud sync, messaging, league scheduling, score tracking, opponent management, or long-term game reports. The active game timeline exists to support confident live operation and undo.

## Future ideas

- Replace roster-order priority in starter Auto-fill with a persistent fairness tie-breaker. A compact per-player rotation ledger could track games attended, games started, the most recent start, and optionally cumulative field time without requiring browsable full-game history. Positional fit would remain primary; among equally suitable players, Auto-fill would favor fewer starts and the least-recent starter before using roster order as the final deterministic fallback.
