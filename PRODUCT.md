# Sideline product truth

## Purpose

Sideline is a mobile-first, installable game-day clipboard for youth recreational soccer coaches. It reduces attention cost during a match: the coach should be able to understand who is playing, who is waiting, how long each player has participated, and what the next fair substitution should be with a glance and a few large taps.

## Users and setting

- A volunteer or recreational coach standing outdoors in bright light, often using one hand.
- Coaches see only their assigned squads; one coach may manage both U8 playing 5v5 and U12 playing 9v9.
- Connectivity is not assumed. There is no account, backend, or cross-device sync.

## Durable behavior

The editable set of upcoming pairings is a **Substitution plan**. **Create plan**, **Add to plan**, and **Update plan** edit those instructions; **In this plan** describes a player's pairing. **Send players in** executes the reviewed set. **Next reminder** describes the timing prompt, not an automatic substitution or permission from the referee. Individual immediate substitutions retain the explicit **Sub now** action.

- Starting both goalkeeper options is valid. Setup does not require a benched reserve or show "No backup goalkeeper" warnings or "GK option" labels. Saved goalkeeper preferences remain visible in player pickers; live rotation planning prepares the next keeper when needed.

- Starter drag feedback appears only while dragging over a valid destination, inset inside the top of the pitch near the opposing goal without moving its positions. It shows proposed role fit for affected players, without bench-reserve warnings. No generic drop instruction appears over empty space. The small name preview remains centered on the pointer independently.

- Starter pitch/bench dragging is bidirectional. Dropping a starter onto a specific bench player puts that bench player in the starter's position and benches the starter atomically, through the same assignment and Undo path as the reverse gesture. Blank bench space and bench-to-bench drops do not change assignments.

- A fresh device begins with a static local coach picker rather than credentials or remote authentication. The selected coach persists locally until **Change coaches** is used. Brian is assigned to Golden Dragons as head coach and Fireballers as assistant coach; Chris is Fireballers head coach; Scott is a Fireballers assistant coach; Lindsey is a Golden Dragons assistant coach.
- Team selection shows only the selected coach’s assigned teams and keeps team state explicitly separate. Coaches assigned to one team skip this screen and enter that team directly. A coach assigned to an active game also resumes it directly, even when assigned to multiple teams; multi-team selection appears only when no authorized game is active. Its helper copy mentions starting a game normally and switches to resuming only while a game is in progress.
- Changing coaches clears only the local coach persona and never clears an active game, roster, preference, or history. While a game is active, coach-picker rows without access to that team remain visible but cannot be activated and name the team preventing entry. A stale persisted coach who is not assigned to the active team cannot resume or overwrite it; the fallback names the active team explicitly.
- The coach picker provides a named **End game** recovery action for the single active match. It requires destructive confirmation, finalizes elapsed time through the normal end-game path, clears the persisted active game, and opens the game summary with **Return to coaches**.
- Confirming **End game** definitively finalizes and removes the persisted active game before showing its summary. Leaving or signing out from that summary must never expose the completed match as resumable.
- The install-to-home-screen action lives on coach selection so it remains available even when a single-team coach normally skips team selection.
- When a game is active, team selection shows its current clock state and live soccer match time in the resume strip rather than a stale snapshot.
- Team names, rosters, and game durations are fixed product data.
- Game setup is a three-step linear flow for attendance, formation (including U8 period format), and starters/bench. Navigation buttons name their destination, and step selections remain intact when moving backward. Attendance tiles sort by player name ascending, then numeric jersey number ascending for matching names (unnumbered players last), independently of attendance status. Each name is paired with a compact jersey-number label for faster visual confirmation. Guests use the same ordering within their separate section. This display order never changes the permanent roster order or starter-selection priority. Attendance below the team’s side size changes the count to a danger state and explicitly states the current count, required count, and shortfall.
- Starter assignment uses the selected formation as a tactics board. Tapping a position opens the present-player picker; choosing an assigned player swaps positions, while choosing a bench player replaces the current starter. Manual choices rank first through fourth position preferences, then unrecorded preferences, then non-matches, with alphabetical ties. Current assignment does not change that order. The Starting bench list remains alphabetical for quick scanning. Auto-fill preserves manually assigned starters and optimizes the remaining present players only for open positions. Initial assignment balances position preferences against anticipated goalkeeper duty: with multiple eligible keepers, an equal share of goalkeeper minutes plus the first outfield rotation is compared with the attendance-based equal playing share. Exceeding that budget adds a soft outfield preference penalty, not a mandatory bench reservation. The first outfield turn uses one eighth of game duration for U8 and one quarter for U12, regardless of period format. Auto-fill is disabled when the board is already full.
- Starter editing supports dragging a pitch player onto another position (swapping occupants or moving into an open role), and dragging the whole starting-bench player button onto the pitch (benching the displaced starter or filling an opening). There is no separate grip. Mouse/pen dragging begins with movement; touch dragging requires a 125ms hold on both pitch and bench players, while moving more than 8px before that hold cancels the pending drag and allows normal page scrolling. The live pitch uses the same hold and scroll protection. A quick tap or keyboard activation selects the bench player, then activating a pitch position places them; existing position pickers remain available. Tapping outside a player, tapping the selected bench player again, or pressing Escape clears selection without a separate Cancel button. Tapping another bench player selects them instead. Dragging highlights the destination and follows the pointer with the player's name; its temporary role-fit feedback stays inside the top of the pitch rather than following the name. Drag move consequences also remain available to screen readers. The picker explains bench replacement versus starter swaps once, rather than repeating arrow-based move breakdowns in every row. Only releasing over a valid position commits; release outside, pointer cancellation, Escape, and window blur cancel. Near the viewport edges, dragging scrolls to reach offscreen positions.
- U8 keeps exactly one previous kickoff lineup locally: the players who started and those available on the bench. It is captured when the clock first starts (or demo time first advances), including the coach's final pre-kickoff changes; merely preparing or abandoning a game does not replace it. Substitutions, injuries, position changes, and clock restarts never rewrite that game's snapshot. Ending a game retains this small record, and the next U8 kickoff replaces it. U12 games neither use nor overwrite it. Players who attended but began the last U8 game on the bench receive a 500-point automatic-selection bonus, half a preference-rank step, rather than a guaranteed start. Absent or new players are neutral. Initial setup, formation changes, Auto-fill, and attendance-based vacancy filling use this history; manual assignments remain authoritative.
- Attendance changes recalculate untouched automatic lineups for both teams using the same preference-aware starter solver as Reset followed by Auto-fill. Removing players in a different order does not change the resulting automatic lineup. Once the coach edits or explicitly fills the board, assignments for still-present players remain fixed and only vacancies are filled by preference. Undo restores whether the previous board was automatic or coach-edited; selecting a formation explicitly rebuilds an automatic lineup.
- Small warning icons mark assigned starter positions with preference mismatches, including an atypical starting goalkeeper. Tapping the player or icon opens the existing starter picker, which explains that position's concerns above replacement choices. These are advisory, not blocks; coaches can override without a confirmation dialog. Empty positions, including goalkeeper, are normal unfinished setup: they retain the Open label without a warning icon or picker warning. Game-start completeness checks remain unchanged. Missing preferences are distinguished from an actual mismatch. There is no persistent warning panel on the pitch. Picker rows show names, preferences, and current assignments, without reserve warnings. Undo lineup change restores the previous assignments and derived bench after a manual move, picker choice, Auto-fill, or Reset; formation or attendance changes invalidate that snapshot. These edits never create live substitution events.
- Formations include an explicit goalkeeper plus the correct number of field positions. Every position has a compact code for spatial surfaces, a medium scan label, and a full descriptive name.
- The game clock can pause and resume. Persisted timestamps allow safe recovery after refresh or relaunch.
- Returning to a running game after Sideline was hidden immediately materializes elapsed time, persists the recovered clock, restores any added-time state, and surfaces an overdue substitution reminder without waiting for the next ordinary timer tick.
- Player field and bench time accrue only while the game clock runs.
- An opt-in, device-persisted **Demo mode** preference adds a fast-forward icon to the expanded live-game header. Clock and End game controls retain their labels above the mobile breakpoint; only mobile demo controls become icon-only. Fast-forward opens a positive-minute offset dialog that first materializes any real elapsed time, uses the same field/bench accounting for the added interval, pauses automatically after the jump, and never changes periods automatically. Its **Current match time** and **Jump to** destination use the same period-aware clock as the live header, not the raw accumulated playing-time counter, so earlier added time does not produce mismatched clock values.
- Fair substitution suggestions prioritize returning players who will have rested approximately one rotation at the planned change time, using the same 20% timing allowance as keeper changes. Within that group, longer current bench-time bands lead, then total playing time and the existing fairness ties. When nobody is ready from the bench, least-played priority still supports late arrivals. After at least one confirmed substitution, automatic suggestions project current Playing and Sitting intervals to the next scheduled rotation window while leaving real totals untouched; planning immediately after sending players in therefore models when the queued plan is expected to be used instead of treating every new entrant as permanently fresh. When several outgoing candidates are reasonably close in playing time, the planner spreads changes across defensive, midfield, and attacking lines and avoids replacing an entire line at once when possible. A substantial playing-time gap still takes precedence over line continuity.
- During live play, U8 uses one eighth of configured game duration in both halves and quarters: a 40-minute game reminds every 5:00, a 48-minute game every 6:00, and a 50-minute game every 6:15. These are prompts for the next permitted stoppage, not automatic substitutions or permission to ignore league substitution rules. U12 always uses one quarter of configured duration, regardless of format (15:00 in its default 60-minute game). The interval is rounded to the nearest second. The timer starts at kickoff or the most recent confirmed planned rotation, including a deliberately sent partial batch. One-off **Sub now** and live bench-drop swaps, like automatic injury/unavailability replacements, preserve that deadline and notification cycle while starting new individual playing/bench stints. Thus a U12 immediate swap or injury replacement at 5:00 leaves a 15:00 deadline intact, whereas a confirmed planned rotation at 14:00 makes the next deadline 29:00. One-off changes while overdue do not replay the chime or reopen a dismissed review. The planner recognizes them as personnel changes but projects rest to the unchanged team-rotation deadline; a just-benched player is not credited a new full rotation of rest. Existing saved immediate swaps are recognized from their original history notes and normalized without changing assignments, time, or undo history. Merely readying a plan does not reset the timer. A ready plan replaces the no-plan reminder banner, but its countdown and due notification remain active. Pauses do not advance the timer, and period-break actions take precedence. Existing games use the interval derived from their saved team and duration without resetting the clock, lineup, history, or queued plans.
- When a running rotation timer crosses into due while Sideline is in the foreground and idle, the app opens the queued-plan review, or the suggested-plan editor when nothing is queued. This behavior has no separate setting and does not depend on sound being enabled. It never changes assignments, confirms substitutions, or pauses the clock. Open dialogs, menus, text entry, and pointer/keyboard interactions take precedence: a blocked or dismissed automatic opening is not retried during that rotation. Restoring an already-overdue game retains the reminder without a surprise dialog.
- Routine rotations stop being recommended when the next scheduled change would leave no more than one fairness timing band of regulation play: three minutes for standard U12 games and two minutes for U8. This uses period-aware remaining time, not raw elapsed time, and does not restart during final added time. The cutoff suppresses the automatic prompt, chime, due banner, and suggested default count; rotation status reads **No more scheduled**. Manual counts and injury replacements remain available, and already-readied plans stay intact and executable.
- Before an intermediate quarter or half ends, a scheduled rotation that would leave no more than that same timing buffer is deferred to the period's regulation boundary. Planning projections and keeper-preparation advice use the break as the intended change time, including actual period starts shifted by earlier added time. This overrides the normal ready-banner countdown and due-alert behavior described above: valid ready-plan banners, due banners, keeper-preparation prompts, sounds, vibration, and automatic review openings stay quiet when no useful rotation remains in the current period. Status reads **At the break** instead of counting down to a last-seconds swap. The sticky Review/Create plan action stays available; queued players, assignments, history, and clocks are never rewritten by deferral. At regulation time and during the paused break, the period banner keeps the ready count and review action. Added time does not reactivate nagging; the coach may still send players in manually. Only an actual confirmed planned change resets the nominal interval, and normal prompting becomes eligible again in the next period. Unlike the final-game cutoff, planning at an intermediate break retains suggested pairs. Invalid saved plans still surface their actionable error.
- Device-local game-day settings optionally keep the screen awake while an active game is visible and play one two-note chime per newly due rotation, with vibration where the browser supports it. Switching Substitution alerts on immediately previews that same chime/vibration; switching off or restoring the preference does not preview it. If a rotation is already due when enabled, the preview counts as its notification rather than playing twice; future deadlines still alert normally. Queued plans and open dialogs do not suppress the due sound. Wake lock is a progressive enhancement: it releases when Sideline is hidden, reacquires on return, reports failure rather than pretending to be active, and remains disabled on unsupported browsers.
- Substitution reminders remain team-level: there is no separate keeper alarm or extra stoppage. The nominal goalkeeper target is twice the team's rounded reminder interval, measured from actual entry into goal, including position changes. A 40-minute U8 game uses a 10:00 keeper target in both halves and quarters; a 50-minute U8 game uses 12:30. A 60-minute U12 game uses 30:00. A primary keeper's first turn retains that nominal target. Repeat turns and backup keepers' first turns may be shortened to one normal rotation when another full turn would consume the player's remaining playing budget or reserved outfield opportunity. Preparation, planner notices, and execution notices use that same adjusted target; they do not warn that an intentionally shorter, on-time turn is early. A short turn's successor is prepared in the handoff batch itself: keep eligible cover resting or bench an outfield keeper, with the usual reservation transfer when another eligible keeper can provide that rest. This may reduce the suggested batch by one when no substitute cover exists; an explicit full-count override remains possible. Early changes remain coach-overridable. Existing saved games derive these targets without resetting assignments, clocks, history, or queued choices.
- Keeper timing and rest warnings in the planner use the same next-rotation deadline as suggestions. Planning a valid U12 minute-30 handoff at minute 15 is not an early change. The ready checklist instead assesses the actual clock: an early execution is explicitly labeled "Wait on this rotation," and insufficient rest is explained for a bench player entering goal. A queued field-to-keeper move deliberately keeps that player on, so live preparation, planner, and review do not ask them to take a bench turn first. Early-change and position-preference cautions still apply. Automatic keeper selection and rest eligibility are unchanged. These advisories update while the checklist is open and never block an explicit coach override. Saving a plan does not execute it or reset keeper tenure.
- Keeper turns and bench turns are targets, not exact deadlines. A stoppage window of 20% of the normal rotation interval, rounded to the nearest second, applies to both: three minutes for standard U12 games and one minute for a 40-minute U8 game in either format. U8's five-minute bench target therefore permits a return after four minutes, without shortening its separate keeper target to a single rotation interval. Suggestions, preparation, and warnings share this tolerance. For U12, a starting keeper can hand over without a timing warning after 27 minutes if the incoming keeper has rested at least 12 minutes; a minute-29 stoppage after a minute-15 bench entry is therefore fine. Genuinely short rest still warns independently. Readiness does not change the nominal targets or reminder schedule, and a confirmed early rotation resets the usual timer from its actual execution time.
- Goalkeeper preparation is recalculated deterministically from actual assignments, bench stints, playing totals, cumulative goalkeeper time, availability, and the next existing rotation opportunity; no stale successor reservation or separate keeper-time counter is persisted. Keeper minutes come from actual position history, including position changes and undo, not projected future service. When a handoff is due, rested candidates rank first. Next, candidates with less projected overage against their total-playing-time budget rank ahead of those with more. Within those constraints, goalkeeper preference order ranks first-choice keepers ahead of second-, third-, and fourth-choice keepers; cumulative keeper-time balancing then applies within a preference rank. Thus moving goalkeeper down a player's preference list reduces automatic keeper priority rather than merely leaving eligibility unchanged. Suggested keeper choices for a coach-selected full-side rotation use the same ranking among rested candidates, while explicitly selected players remain authoritative. Among similarly ranked and used keepers, prefer one who has already received their outfield opportunity so another can return outfield rather than remain reserved for goal. Time comparisons use existing fairness bands before projected workload, bench status, exact keeper/workload totals, and stable player-ID tie-breaks. Projected workload includes anticipated outfield time before rest, the budgeted next goalkeeper stint capped by remaining regulation, and any outstanding outfield allowance. The allowance is up to one rotation, limited to the playing budget left after the larger of a nominal keeper turn and an equal share of goal coverage among available keeper options. This avoids promising impossible outfield time with too few keepers. A selected outfield successor is prioritized OUT one rotation before handoff, then held for a full bench rotation. Before that preparation window, a benched successor may play outfield only if the extra interval plus forthcoming keeper duty fits their attendance-based playing budget within a fairness band. Other goalkeeper options are not automatically withheld. Preference rank is a priority, not a fixed quota or a guarantee that every eligible keeper plays in goal; readiness, total-playing fairness, and coach overrides still take precedence.
- A rested keeper reservation can transfer within a batch: the benched keeper may return outfield when that same suggested rotation benches another eligible keeper who has had a normal field turn and whose cumulative keeper-time band is no greater than theirs. This prevents replacing an unused keeper's opportunity with an already more-used keeper. The outgoing selection must include that replacement so returning the reserved player does not silently lose the next keeper's rest. If no replacement is available, keeper cover remains protected. Live preparation guidance evaluates the whole queued batch and its available rest time, rather than warning solely because the previously preferred successor is going outfield. A genuinely unsafe or late plan still receives guidance; queued choices are never rewritten.
- At handoff, the planner chooses a rested eligible keeper. A missed rest or coach override delays the suggested takeover rather than sending a tired outfielder straight into a long keeper turn. An on-field successor can still participate in an atomic linked handoff if they just entered (less than one fairness band) after a full bench rotation: the current keeper goes OUT, the successor MOVES to GK, and a bench player comes IN at the vacated outfield position. Injuries, partial rotations, position moves, and late arrivals trigger recomputation while preserving normal reminder semantics and explicit queued pairs. Upcoming handoff warnings appear only when preparation is at risk, another keeper is unavailable, or no bench rest is possible; without a bench, coaches can use Change positions rather than reducing the side. No handoff is prepared beyond regulation.
- Substitution planning starts from the goalkeeper-coverage maximum and includes the eligible rested returners up to field capacity. An aggregate playing-time gap must not cut that group down and leave rested children benched for a second cycle. For remaining fresh candidates, the existing two-band Played/Sitting gap can still reduce the default, while a required goalkeeper change remains included. The default, count selector, and suggestion generator share the same outgoing-position capacity: protecting the current keeper limits an automatic U8 plan to four swaps even with five on the bench; a direct keeper replacement permits five. A linked goalkeeper handoff reserves the moving player's field position, so that player cannot also be suggested OUT when expanding the plan. Existing explicit keeper overrides remain editable. This prevents impossible counts from producing an empty plan while making full rested-bench rotations the ordinary case. Changing the requested swap count re-optimizes untouched suggestions; once the coach has selected or readied specific pairs, count changes preserve those choices and add or trim only the surrounding suggestions.
- The multi-player planner uses a compact IN / OUT ledger. Player selectors stay concise while a separate status line shows the incoming player’s current bench stint and total playing time and the outgoing player’s current field stint and total playing time. IN choices rank least total playing time first, then longest current bench stint; OUT choices rank longest current field stint first, then greatest total playing time. Both use player name as the final tie-breaker. Players selected in another row remain visible but cannot be selected again in the same IN or OUT column, preventing duplicate plans before validation is needed. Every editable row keeps the affected position and substitution arrow between the columns plus an explicit remove control. Removing a row preserves every other selected pair, renumbers the plan, and lowers the requested swap count. The row itself is the complete planning summary; no duplicate confirmation ledger follows it.
- Planner player menus use one direct question rather than a title plus explanatory subtitle. The IN menu asks only **Who should go in?** because neither the current destination nor outgoing player affects the bench choice; both remain independently adjustable afterward. The OUT menu asks **Where should [incoming player] play?**, matching its position-selection purpose.
- Live substitution work is organized IN first, then destination, then OUT. The incoming player leads every planner and review row because the coach’s primary sideline task is getting the right player into the right position; the outgoing player remains explicit but supporting. Planner rows and the dedicated ready-review sheet use the medium scan label (for example, **Left Mid**) above the arrow, where the pitch’s spatial context is absent. The wider position lane deliberately takes space from the player selectors, whose names remain concise and directly editable.
- The count selector keeps all bench/field-sized options visible. When the default recommendation keeps the goalkeeper on, choosing the full-side count (5 for U8) explicitly includes that keeper in one step. Untouched suggestions are recalculated as a complete batch with an eligible replacement keeper; changing the count alone never freezes pairings. Once the coach manually changes player choices or readies a plan, those pairings are preserved, any remaining outfield swaps are filled, and the keeper goes OUT for the remaining bench player IN. There is no separate override link or manual setup sequence. The default remains keeper-protective, including four-player U8 recommendations before keeper relief is due; coaches can still prepare a full opening rotation immediately. Focus moves to the keeper row's IN selector so it can be adjusted normally. An early keeper change is flagged only when a keeper swap is actually in the plan, and that warning clears once the recommended keeper stint is complete. Smaller outfield-only plans do not show keeper messages. A fresh automatic full-team calculation requires a keeper-capable player on the bench; otherwise it leaves the current plan intact and explains that the keeper pairing needs a manual choice. Preserved coach choices that put a player outside goalkeeper preferences remain explicitly flagged. Count changes only edit the draft; the usual Ready and Send players in actions still apply. Full-side counts remain disabled for linked goalkeeper handoffs because the moving player must remain on the field.
- A confirmed substitution is atomic: no duplicate assignment, no player both on field and bench, and no accidental change in the valid field count.
- A linked keeper handoff is shown last in the planner and ready checklist as one horizontal row, naming all three players and their destinations. The change-positions icon and **Keeper** sit beneath the moving player's name; substitution icons accompany the incoming player's outfield destination and outgoing player's **Bench** destination. The review description separates **N substitutions + 1 position move** and says the moving player stays on at keeper; the editable planner explains the resulting swap-count limit beside its controls. The routes are **IN: bench → vacated outfield position**, **MOVE: outfield position → Keeper**, **OUT: Keeper → bench**. Full routes are announced accessibly; visible destination labels keep the checklist compact. It never implies the bench entrant directly replaces the outgoing keeper at the outfield position. The incoming selector stays editable; removal discards all three linked actions together. The move remains pending until **Send players in**. Ordinary keeper timing, recommendation counts, and coach overrides are unchanged.
- A ready plan's live banner and period summaries include the named keeper move. The future keeper's pitch marker retains their actual current position and adds **→ Keeper**; the On field row says **Moving to Keeper · stays on**. The exiting keeper names who takes goal, and the bench entrant is labeled with the vacated outfield destination rather than incorrectly saying GK. Tapping any of those three players' plan controls opens the existing full handoff review, not a two-player picker that would obscure the linked move. Editing still uses **Edit plan**. Pending indicators follow the saved plan, survive reload, and disappear when the handoff is removed or executed. The existing undo behavior is unchanged.
- Focused pickers protect all three participants in a saved linked keeper change. Selecting its moving player, exiting keeper, or incoming bench player routes planning to **Edit keeper plan** rather than splitting the operation; the domain also rejects a focused edit that would split it. The conflict explanation appears beside the actions after selection, without a separate full-plan notice above the choices. Ordinary full-plan replacements use **Replace planned swap** without changing the handoff. Any immediate substitution while a linked handoff is saved—including a bench drag—requires **Sub now and replan** confirmation because immediate swaps recalculate the remaining plan. Cancelling leaves both lineup and plan unchanged; ordinary immediate swaps without a saved handoff remain one-step actions.
- After a planned substitution or an automatic replacement for an on-field player taken out of the game, a substitution-ready sheet shows the numbered IN / OUT pairs and affected positions so the coach can organize players before play resumes.
- The latest confirmed substitution or out-of-game event can be undone.
- The live game timeline is hidden until a real game event exists, then remains collapsed by default to protect live-screen space. Starting or resuming the clock and adding structural quarter/half markers do not reveal a zero-event timeline. Undoing the only game event hides it again. Expanding it shows the affected player on out-of-game and return events and the OUT / IN players on substitutions. Every known event type has a stable scan icon: clock for period transitions, flag for the final whistle, reverse arrows for substitutions, four-way Move for position changes, soccer ball for goals, plus for returning players, and player-out for unavailable players. Structural quarter or half markers divide the event stream using soccer match timestamps: the first period starts at `0:00`, later-period markers begin at their nominal regulation boundary while pairing the newly started period with the prior period’s actual endpoint and added time, and the completed-game timeline marks the final whistle. These markers are not undoable game changes. The completed-game summary retains its period and final-whistle record even when no game events were recorded.
- Position changes do not count as substitutions. Tapping an occupied player magnet opens the same **Plan out** picker as tapping that player's On field row, without an intermediate action sheet. Bench-player planning uses the corresponding **Plan in** language. Change positions and Take out of game remain separate controls on the On field row. Choosing Change positions opens an editor fixed to that player with current-position context and large “Swap with” player/position choice rows matching the substitution pickers; the player cannot be changed inside the editor. Its confirmation remains disabled until a destination is selected, then reiterates the exact operation as **Swap with [player]** or **Move to [position]** before applying it. Dragging a magnet onto another position moves or swaps the players directly.
- Position changes from either interaction are recorded in the game timeline and can be undone as the latest event.
- The live game tracks a simple score. A coach records an own-team goal by choosing from the current outfield players (the active goalkeeper is omitted), or records an opponent goal without additional details. Goals carry the live timestamp, appear in the game timeline, participate in undo, persist with the active game, and produce the final score on the game summary.
- The live Goal action and Opponent scored action use Sideline's soccer-ball glyph rather than a generic add icon; the adjacent text distinguishes opening the scorer picker from recording an opponent goal. Individual scorer choices omit a leading icon so soccer balls within those rows exclusively communicate goals already scored.
- Each scorer choice pairs the jersey-numbered player name with their current position abbreviation in a compact neutral Label at the far edge. The Label prevents the code from reading as part of the name without increasing button height, while the accessible name includes the full position. Within this constrained grid, hat tricks compact to one hatted ball plus `×3`; roomier player markers retain all three hatted balls.
- Tapping the own-team score in either live header opens a compact scorer breakdown with one ball marker per recorded goal.
- During a game, the same ball markers directly follow scorer names across pitch magnets and roomy bench, out-of-game, scoring, position, and substitution rows. Dialog titles remain plain jersey-numbered identity and task labels so goal history never competes with navigation hierarchy. One or two goals use individual ball markers; an exact hat trick uses three hatted balls; higher totals collapse to one hatted ball plus the exact count. Native select options remain text-only where markers cannot render reliably.
- Match-level Start/Pause/Resume and End game controls live together in the expanded header and as adjacent icon controls in the collapsed header. Before the clock has first run, both header states use the plain-language **Start game** action; later period breaks retain specific actions such as **Start Q2** or **Start H2**. The mobile control dock contains exactly three equal actions ordered **Undo**, **Create plan**, and **Goal**, keeping substitutions in the central thumb position. Position changes remain immediately accessible by dragging players on the pitch or using Change positions on their On field rows, so a separate Positions dock command is unnecessary.
- The primary live clock follows soccer’s continuous count-up convention across the whole match. Each later quarter or half begins at its nominal regulation boundary, so added time from an earlier period does not shift the displayed start of the next one; current-period added time still advances beyond that period’s nominal endpoint. A clock glyph sits beside the large value; the adjacent Start/Pause/Resume control communicates whether it is running, so the display does not repeat a textual clock-state badge. The period status separately shows time remaining in the current period and switches to an explicit `+m:ss added` state after that period’s regulation target. Actual accumulated active-play time remains authoritative for player totals, substitution fairness, chronological event ordering, and the final report.
- The expanded header names only the current period (`Quarter 2` or `Half 1`) without a redundant total-period denominator. On phones, it compacts into one sticky match-status row while scrolling, preserving the team, a concise `Q2` or `H1` marker, nominal soccer match time, score, and match controls. The ordinary period countdown is omitted from this constrained row; a secondary `+m:ss added` line appears only when the current period has exceeded regulation.
- Configured duration defines regulation time rather than a hard clock limit. At the end of a quarter or half, the clock, player field and bench totals, substitution cadence, goals, and timeline continue into explicitly labeled added time until the coach ends the period. The persistent period prompt absorbs substitution planning or ready-plan review so it does not compete with a second reminder banner. Ending an intermediate period materializes time and pauses the clock; the header and break banner then explicitly offer **Start** for the next quarter or half. Starting it establishes its regulation deadline from the actual restart, so added time shifts later boundaries without shortening later periods. At the final boundary, play continues in added time until the coach confirms End game. Refresh and relaunch preserve the active period, its actual start, and any overrun without truncating accumulated time. If an older recovery record lacks explicit period state, Sideline stays in the last acknowledged period rather than guessing from total elapsed time and accidentally skipping ahead.
- Taking any player out of the game requires confirmation. Confirming a bench player removes them from availability; confirming an on-field player then uses the fairest available bench replacement and shows the resulting change, or leaves the position explicitly open when no replacement exists.
- Players not marked as attending begin in the Out of game section rather than disappearing. A late arrival can be added to the game and joins the bench, or fills an open position automatically when the team is short-sided.
- During attendance, the coach can add named guest players borrowed for that match at any time, whether the team is short-sided or simply needs more substitutes. Adding a guest means they are present for this game; guest rows do not duplicate the permanent roster’s present/absent toggle, and removal is the single way to undo an addition before kickoff. The entry accepts an optional jersey number. Guests appear in a separate temporary section, participate in assignment, timing, substitutions, scoring, persistence, and the final report, but never enter the team’s permanent roster and disappear when the game is closed.
- If fewer than the regulation side size are available, assigning every available player enables an explicitly confirmed short-sided start rather than blocking the game.
- During a live game, the coach can add a game-only guest from the compact plus control beside the Bench count. The player joins the bench, their bench time begins at that moment, and the addition can be undone without counting as a substitution. During a short-sided game, tapping an open pitch position remains a shortcut that creates a guest directly in that position and starts their playing time immediately.
- The live Out of game list is absent when every player is available. Once populated, it is collapsed by default, describes its members as not currently available to play, shows its count in the summary row, and expands when a coach needs to add a player to the game.
- Both teams use an attendance-aware minimum playing-time pace: the smaller of 50% and 80% of the equal share (field places, including goalkeeper, divided by currently available attendees). Absent and unavailable players are excluded, and active guests are included. U8 floors are 40% with ten players, about 44.4% with nine, and 50% with eight or fewer; U12 uses 48% with fifteen, 50% with fourteen or fewer, and 40% with eighteen. Injury, return, and guest events recalculate the current floor. Each player's minimum uses their actual accumulated field plus bench time, not time since kickoff, so late arrival, pauses, and time out of the game are excluded. The percentage is applied to all of that player's available time using current attendance; it is not a historical attendance-weighted quota. These existing counters survive refresh and undo without new persisted fairness state.
- A cumulative deficit greater than the minimum playing time for one rotation plus one minute triggers a persistent match-level **Players need more time** notice. This allows normal rotation-sized swings without restarting the allowance each time a player enters or leaves the field. There is no quarter-game delay and no requirement to be currently benched; queued plans never count as played or suppress the warning. The notice remains available in either roster tab, shows actual played minutes and approximate additional minutes needed by full time, and provides **Plan in** or **Review plan** for bench players. On-field players are advised to keep playing to catch up. Currently unavailable players are not prompted to enter.
- The full-time minimum projects current attendance and availability through the period-aware remaining regulation time, including actual added-time offsets. A deficit becomes time-sensitive when waiting for the next scheduled rotation risks leaving too little time to catch up, or when only a minute of spare regulation time remains beyond the needed playing time. The latter also surfaces smaller deficits that fit within the normal rotation allowance, but ignores shortfalls of up to and including 60 seconds against the minimum accrued so far. This avoids urgent alerts for tiny differences hidden by rounded minute displays. This safeguard remains visible during routine period-end deferral and final-rotation cutoff; it does not open dialogs, make sounds, change the rotation timer, or execute substitutions. Warnings clear as actual accumulated time catches up. The minimum percentage does not tighten near full time, and the planner's fairness objective is unchanged.
- Every End game confirmation, including coach-picker recovery, checks actual played time against the minimum for time available so far. It lists underplayed players and their minimums without the live rotation allowance, but with the same inclusive 60-second shortfall tolerance as the late-game alert. The tolerance filters advisories; it does not lower the calculated minimum or change player totals. Ending early does not charge players for unplayed future minutes. The check continues updating while the clock runs. Continue/Keep game and Close preserve the game, while an explicit **End game** still finishes when play is over.
- Bench rows prioritize identity and relevant timing exceptions. Players who have all sat since kickoff share one exact cohort timer above the list instead of repeating the game clock in every row; divergent sitting times return to the affected rows in `m:ss` below one minute and rounded values such as `4 min` afterward. Players with no accumulated field time read “Not played yet.” **Scheduled in at [position] for [player]** and the below-pace warning (actual percentage to at most one decimal) remain visible together when both apply. Otherwise, distinct aggregate bench time is phrased compactly as **22 min bench**. Planned on-field players read **Scheduled out for [player]**, and aggregate field history uses **44 min played**. Pending statuses and planned-out pitch magnets use the reverse-arrows substitution icon; checkmarks remain reserved for selected or confirmed choices. Redundant bench totals stay hidden. Tapping a player's number, name, or timing opens substitution planning or edits their plan. Bench rows retain Take out of game; field rows expose Change positions and Take out of game separately. Removal requires confirmation. With no substitutes, field-row planning is disabled while positions and removal remain accessible.
- The live roster area has accessible Bench and On field tabs, defaults to Bench, and uses tap/click or keyboard navigation rather than horizontal swipes. Bench rows sort by longest current sitting time first; On field rows sort by longest current playing time first. Equal times retain their prior bench or formation order for stability. On-field rows show position context and initially suppress **Playing** timing while every player’s current uninterrupted turn merely duplicates the main game clock. Once any current playing time diverges, every on-field row and pitch magnet gains comparable numeric timing in `m:ss` below one minute and rounded values such as `4 min` afterward. Pitch magnets deliberately show current **Playing** time rather than aggregate **Played** time because the field is the immediate rest-and-rotation surface; aggregate fairness remains available in both roster lists and player dialogs. Pitch magnets also add the substitution swap icon beside the position code when that player is ready to come out. Their **Plan out** action starts with the outgoing player and asks which bench player should enter. Tapping a pitch player directly opens the same Plan out picker as the On field row, including existing queued-pair selection and context. With no bench players, the pitch picker explains that a player must be added or returned and disables substitution actions; pitch dragging and the row controls for positions and removal remain available.
- Live bench rows support dragging onto an occupied pitch position as a shortcut to **Sub now**, using the same atomic swap, smaller-plan refresh, and undo behavior. Touch uses the shared 125ms hold; movement before the hold remains normal scrolling. Phones and short windows show a temporary drop pitch without collapsing or scrolling the detailed bench rows. Wider screens use the existing pitch and a compact status card near its upper-left corner, lifted above the first player targets when needed to avoid covering them. A valid drop must match the last previewed target; release elsewhere, Escape, multitouch, resize, blur, or a changed lineup cancels without changing the game. Normal row taps, keyboard planning, and separate Take out controls remain available.
- When a player added to the game fills an open position, a player-ready sheet identifies the numbered player and their assigned position before play continues.
- Ending a game pauses and materializes the clock, scrolls the report to the top, then shows a per-player summary sorted with scorers first and remaining players by total playing time. It includes every position played, time at each position, total playing time, and total goals. The game timeline records the position occupied for each goal alongside its exact scoring time. The same confirmed-event timeline appears below the player summary in a collapsed disclosure.
- Substitution planning is a persistent two-phase workflow. Readying a plan does not change the lineup or timers; the live screen keeps the ready plan visible and editable until the coach sends every swap in atomically at the actual substitution time. Suggestions are projected when generated, then remain a stable coach-approved snapshot rather than silently re-optimizing while queued. A ready swap belongs to its OUT / IN player pairing: if the outgoing player changes position before the substitution, the plan follows that player to the new position without changing who goes out or in. Undoing the position change restores the prior planned position. The review screen can remove individual swaps without creating empty replacement rows: removing one reduces the intended plan size by one, and removing the final swap deletes the plan. Goal markers are deliberately omitted from this operational checklist so names, jersey numbers, direction, and position remain the only staging information. Plans made impossible by other lineup changes remain visible but cannot be sent in until repaired or cancelled.
- Player selectors inside the multi-substitution planner use the same row anatomy as focused Plan in and Plan out dialogs. IN choices show numbered identity beside explicit preferred roles with Bench / Played timing; they omit ordinal fit because **Who should go in?** is intentionally independent of the row’s changeable destination. OUT choices lead with jersey-numbered identity and preference rank, then show current position with Playing / Total timing. Focused Plan in and planner OUT destination lists share one deterministic order: unassigned positions first; rest-due players whose current Playing time reaches the configured rotation interval; normal players; then fresh players below one completed game-scaled fairness band. Within the same rest zone, aggregate Played-time bands lead, with a one-band adjustment for outside-preference destinations, followed by current Playing-time bands, ordinal preference rank, exact timing, and formation order. The band is 20% of the game’s recommended rotation interval, rounded to a whole minute with a two-minute minimum—two minutes for default U8 games and three minutes for default U12 games. This prevents insignificant clock differences from reshuffling recommendations, protects someone who just entered, and promotes someone who has played continuously for too long without abandoning whole-game fairness. Every OUT position remains visible: positions already assigned to another row move after unplanned choices, show **Scheduled out for [incoming player]**, and selecting one swaps the two rows’ OUT destinations so the plan remains complete and duplicate-free. Incoming players already used elsewhere remain visible with concise reverse-arrows **Scheduled in for [player]** status, sort after unplanned choices, and can be safely transferred between swaps. Focused pickers retain pairing context for transferable choices in either direction.
- When planning a bench player IN, outgoing choices lead with the current player’s jersey number and name, with their full current position as secondary context. The planner’s **Who’s coming OUT?** menu uses the same player-first hierarchy. Preference labels, playing-time metrics, and destination sorting are unchanged. Planning a single on-field player OUT also keeps the incoming player’s name primary.
- Each available bench player has a direct plan-in action. Its title explicitly identifies that the player is going in, and a responsive fact grid shows **Sitting now**, **Played**, preferred roles under the concise **Positions** label, and the planned pairing when one already exists. Its outgoing-player choices show position, **Playing** time for the current uninterrupted turn, and total played so the coach can compare workload before selecting who comes off. On-field variants, including the plan-out and change-position dialogs, use the same grid with separate **Position**, **Played**, and **Playing now** facts rather than combining multiple times in one sentence. All player-specific time facts and choice-row metrics use `m:ss` only below one minute, then stable rounded values such as `9 min`, preventing live dialogs from visually changing every second. A zero lifetime playing total reads **Not played yet**; active playing and sitting timers retain `0:00` because they begin counting when play resumes. They also show the same conditional **In this plan** pairing, so moving a player never hides relevant ready-plan context. Focused plan-in, plan-out, and change-position dialogs use the same select-then-confirm interaction: identity opposite role fit or destination, then compact labeled timing beneath. Plan-out candidate rows use their lower-left space for a distinct **PREFERS** label and role value, explaining the ordinal or outside-preferences result while bench and played time remain aligned opposite. A single preferred role remains spelled out; multiple roles compact to **GK / DEF / MID / FWD** abbreviations while assistive text retains the full names. Preference matches retain explicit ordinal labels and add a restrained rank tint: green for first, gold for second, orange for third, rust for fourth, and red for **Outside preferences**. The words remain the primary signal, so color never carries the meaning alone. Selecting a destination in Change positions previews the Card value as the current full position, a swap arrow, and the destination abbreviation before confirmation. Its disabled footer instructs **Choose a position**, then becomes the specific **Swap with…** or **Move to…** action. Tapping a row previews the selection without changing persistent game state; an explicit footer action commits it, and the header close control cancels. Open destination positions remain sparse and explicitly say **Unoccupied position**. Selected rows use a checkmark, turf surface, and structural inset in addition to `aria-pressed`. **Add to plan** or **Update plan** starts a ready plan or updates that player within the existing ready batch without rebuilding unrelated swaps, then returns directly to the live game rather than opening the full checklist. Review remains available explicitly or through the guarded automatic opening at the next rotation deadline.
- Both single-player substitution pickers offer a secondary **Sub now** action after a replacement is selected, including an unchanged existing pairing. It executes only that chosen swap at the current clock time, leaves the outgoing player available on the bench, and never creates a plan if none existed. If a plan existed, Sideline regenerates its recommendations with at most the previous number of swaps minus one, reruns recommended sizing (including keeper constraints and the finishing cutoff), and excludes the newly benched player from that refreshed batch. An empty remainder clears the plan. The result sheet reports the immediate change and refreshed-plan outcome. Undo restores the original lineup and pre-swap plan, including after refresh/relaunch. Unlike Take out of game, this does not mark a player unavailable or simply filter invalid pairs from the old plan.
- Coach-selected single substitutions do not warn that the incoming keeper must sit a full rotation. Automatic rotation recommendations still consider keeper preparation and bench time. The immediate action retains current-time early-keeper-turn cautions and a compact, visually title-free warning for a player who does not typically play goalkeeper; these are advisory and do not block the coach's choice.
- Wherever jersey numbers are displayed, the player name leads and the optional number follows as secondary metadata: **Simon #10**. Numbers do not use filled boxes or colored badges. Bench rows use a narrow, vertically centered decorative grip before the name; the entire row remains draggable. Field-list rows have no grip or leading number column and align their content to the left.
- Individual player dialogs lead with the same name-first identity. **Plan in**, **Plan out**, and **Change position** appear beside it as smaller, quieter task subtitles, wrapping underneath when needed rather than competing with the name in a bold hyphenated title. Context Cards contain only game-state facts and never repeat the player name.
- Taking a ready bench player out of the game automatically removes that player’s swap while preserving every unaffected pair in the plan. Undoing the change restores the prior plan.
- Ending an active game requires confirmation. Its safe action is **Continue game**, and both that action and the standard close button dismiss the dialog without changing the game.
- The home screen offers installation whenever Sideline is running in a browser. Supported browsers open their native install prompt; iPhone and iPad users receive concise Add to Home Screen instructions.

In the focused Plan in and Plan out pickers, tapping a candidate only previews the pairing. **Add to plan / Update plan** use the same replacement behavior: remove existing pairings involving either chosen player and save the new pairing, preserving unrelated swaps. Former partners are not automatically paired together; combining players from two existing pairings reduces the plan by one swap. The scheduled-choice group includes every saved pairing, including the pairing being edited; its membership does not change when previewing another choice. The In this plan preview explicitly names every player who will no longer be in the plan after saving, identifying who stays on the field and who stays on the bench. **Sub now** is separate: it executes the selected substitution immediately and regenerates a smaller remaining plan.

## Data and privacy

All data is stored in browser `localStorage`. Roster and game state use a versioned key; light/dark appearance and game-day device preferences use separate keys. Sideline does not transmit names or game data. Clearing browser site data removes the data and preferences.

## MVP boundaries

The MVP does not include authentication, a backend, cloud sync, messaging, league scheduling, score tracking, opponent management, or long-term game reports. The active game timeline exists to support confident live operation and undo.

## Future ideas

- Replace roster-order priority in starter Auto-fill with a persistent fairness tie-breaker. A compact per-player rotation ledger could track games attended, games started, the most recent start, and optionally cumulative field time without requiring browsable full-game history. Positional fit would remain primary; among equally suitable players, Auto-fill would favor fewer starts and the least-recent starter before using roster order as the final deterministic fallback.
